# How Run Escape plays

Everything in the game, map by map and system by system. The [README](../README.md) has the short
version and how to run it.

- **WASD** or the arrow keys to move. Weapons fire on their own. **1-4** use your utility items.
  **Esc** pauses. **Q** shows or hides the quest tracker. **Tab**, or clicking the DPS under the
  minimap, shows each weapon's damage a second and its total for the run. The mouse wheel zooms.
- On a touch screen, drag anywhere to move: the stick appears under your thumb. Tap the buttons on
  screen to pause and use items, with the thumb still on the stick if you like. Tap the DPS for each
  weapon's damage. Phones play sideways, with gear and quests in the pause menu.
- Your **revives** show beside your active effects, without a timer: a **Ring of life** from the
  shop, and the Pyromancer's own as a **Phoenix**. The phoenix is used first, and each icon goes
  once it's saved you. In co-op they're your own, not a teammate's.
- Enemies drop **bones**; walk near them to collect their experience. Bones lying close together
  gather into one pile worth all of their experience, and it looks the part as it grows: Big
  bones, Dragon bones, Wyvern bones, Lava dragon bones, then Superior dragon bones. Coins gather
  into bigger stacks the same way. With too many piles of either lying around, the one furthest
  away comes to the newest drop, so nothing left far behind is lost. Levels follow the OSRS experience table (stretched for the
  later levels), and each one offers a choice of three: a new weapon, a weapon upgrade or a
  passive. **1-4** pick with the keyboard. **R** rerolls the offer (one reroll a run, more from
  the Pirate and the shop), and **X** skips it for coins from the run: 25 the first time, then
  50, 100, 200 and 400, doubling each time.
- When a run ends, the results screen shows its stats: damage, kills and damage a second for
  each weapon (and the utility items and power-ups that did damage), the biggest hit, damage
  taken, hitpoints healed and when each boss went down. In co-op it has a page for each player,
  flipped through with the arrows or the arrow keys, and the party's shared totals under them. A
  weapon's damage a second counts up to when its player went down or left.
- You carry at most **4 weapons and 4 passives**, so a build is about what you leave out.
- **Rarity**, in OSRS drop table terms: every weapon and passive is Common, Uncommon, Rare or
  Very rare, which sets how often it's offered. Every offer also rolls its own rarity: a Common
  Piety adds 5% damage, a Very rare one 17.5%; rarer weapon upgrades add bonus damage to that
  weapon. Luck (Ring of wealth, the Slayer skill) makes rarer rolls likelier.
- **Weapons** upgrade the OSRS way: scimitars and 2h swords go bronze to dragon, Fire Strike
  becomes Bolt, Blast and Wave, throwing knives go bronze to rune, and shortbows go through the
  woods to magic while their arrows go through the metals. Thrownaxes go bronze to dragon, and
  each one bounces on from what it hits to the next enemy nearby, more times as they upgrade.
  Elemental runes circle you for a while, hitting what they pass and knocking it back, one more
  rune (air, water, earth, fire) every other level. A maxed scimitar with Piety evolves
  into the Abyssal whip; maxed Ice Burst with Protect from Melee becomes Ice Barrage. The weapon in your
  hand and passives like the Graceful outfit or Amulet of glory show up on your character.
- **The Ancient bursts**: Ice Burst freezes what's around you, Smoke Burst poisons it, Shadow
  Burst makes it take 20% more damage for a while and Blood Burst now and then heals you by 2.
  Level ups only offer Ice Burst. Beat the Desert and a burst's second level lets you pick which of
  the four you want, keeping its level, and it's your only burst for that run.
- **Evolutions** from the new maps' passives: maxed elemental runes with Vengeance become
  **Astral runes**, which never stop circling; maxed throwing knives with Obsidian armour become
  **Toktz-xil-ul**, obsidian rings that fly through everything; maxed Saradomin Strike with the
  Book of the dead becomes **Undead Grasp**, whose hands hold what they hit; and a maxed dragon 2h
  sword with Dizana's quiver becomes the **Colossal blade**, which hits bosses and elites again.
  The shortbow with **Rigour** becomes the **Dark bow**, the fire spells with **Augury**
  **Flames of Zamorak**, the thrownaxes with the Tome of fire **Chain-hit axes**, Blood Burst with
  Rapid Heal **Blood Barrage**, Smoke and Shadow Burst with the **Ancient sceptre** **Smoke** and
  **Shadow Barrage**, the Ivandis flail with the Book of the dead the **Blisterwood flail** and the
  Crystal bow with the Graceful outfit the **Corrupted bow**.
- **Class weapon evolutions**: the Scythe of Vitur with **Chivalry** becomes the **Sanguine Scythe
  of Vitur**, which sweeps wider and heals every fourth sweep; a godsword with **Piety** becomes the
  **Ancient godsword**, whose Blood Sacrifice hits all round and heals; the Dragon claws with the
  Tome of fire become the **Burning claws**; the Dragon warhammer with Protect from Melee becomes the
  **Elder maul**, whose Shockwave weakens everything around you; Dinh's bulwark with Vengeance becomes
  **Dinh's blazing bulwark**, whose Shield Bash reaches further and burns; the Twisted bow with Rigour
  becomes the **Corrupted twisted bow**; Tumeken's shadow with Augury becomes the **Corrupted
  tumeken's shadow**; the Sanguinesti staff with Rapid Heal becomes the **Holy sanguinesti staff**;
  and the Harmonised nightmare staff with the Amulet of glory becomes the **Eldritch nightmare staff**,
  whose surges split where they hit.
- **Evolving**: a weapon at its last level with its passive (at any level) offers its evolution first
  on every level up. An evolved weapon has three levels of its own and keeps the weapon's bonus
  damage. Most other class weapons, like the Toxic blowpipe and the Thunder khopesh, don't evolve.
  Level up cards say what evolves with what, the HUD and pause menu show how close a weapon is and
  its slot glows gold once it's ready, and the **Collection log** on the title screen lists every
  recipe, keeping what it makes as ??? until you've made it. It has a tab for each place with
  finds of its own (the Hallowed Night, the Theatre of Blood, the Tombs of Amascut, Justiciar
  Zachariah, Phosani's Nightmare): the weapons it brings in, then its trophies.
- **Style prayers**: **Chivalry** makes slashes and sweeps hit harder and adds max hitpoints for
  fighting up close, **Rigour** makes arrows, bolts and thrown weapons hit harder, and
  **Augury** makes spells hit harder and their effects last longer.
- **Adventurers**: three to start with, each with their own starting weapon, outfit and bonus.
  The rest join for beating a map or for a task done on one (in a single run, however it ends);
  the adventurer screen says what each one wants, and a task done mid-run rings the town
  crier's bell. One can be made a favourite (`SaveData.favouriteCharacter`), and the game starts
  on them.

- **Traits**: Act II's adventurers have a trait, a way to play, rather than a bonus. The
  **Forester**'s is **Timber!**: every 25 kills his next swing fells a Strangled Tree along the
  ground ahead of him, 8 tiles long, for four times the swing's damage, and his swings cut roots 2
  tiles further out than they reach. The **Shadow walker**'s is **Step between**: once every 30
  seconds, a hit that would take over a fifth of her hitpoints takes her into the shadows instead,
  a second and a half out of reach and 3 tiles on the way she faces; in the Lassar Undercity's
  city it takes her (and her party) into the Shadow Realm. The **Treasure hunter**'s is **Dig
  site**: a casket left on the ground buries itself after 3 seconds; walked over again from 20
  seconds it's a tier better, and at 60 seconds it's lost.
- **Class weapons**: every adventurer fights with Melee, Ranged or Magic, and only they can be
  offered their class's weapons. Each class has
  a Very rare one, offered in about one run in twenty, and Rare ones. A new weapon is always
  offered at its own rarity or better, so these come with bonus damage, and the rarest weapon
  you carry is the one in your hand.
    - **Melee**: the **Scythe of Vitur** sweeps a wide arc and hits bosses and elites three
      times (100%, 50%, 25%). The **godswords** are heavy two-handed sweeps, and every third
      swing that has something to hit is their special attack: Armadyl's The Judgement hits
      twice as hard, Bandos's Warstrike makes what it hits take 25% more damage, Saradomin's
      Healing Blade heals you by half the damage, and Zamorak's Ice Cleave freezes. You only
      carry one godsword, and the four share one weapon's chance of being offered. From the
      new maps: **Osmumten's fang**, fast stabs that hit bosses and elites again; the
      **Tzhaar-ket-om**, a slow sweep with huge knockback whose every fourth swing is a Quake
      that stuns; the **Dragon warhammer**, whose every third blow is a Smash that makes what it
      hits take 30% more damage; the **Inquisitor's mace**, crushing blows a quarter harder on the
      undead that hit bosses and elites again; the **Dual macuahuitl**, very fast slashes on both
      sides; and
      the **Thunder khopesh**, quick slashes whose every third is a Thunderclap that hits
      everything around you; **Dinh's bulwark**, a slow bash that hits everything around
      you, further out than any other melee weapon; and the **Keris partisan**, quick stabs that
      hit kalphites and scarabs (Kephri too) a third harder. Each jewel from the Tombs of
      Amascut's chest brings in a keris of its own: **of breaching** now and then finds a gap in
      the shell for triple damage, far more often in kalphites and scarabs; **of corruption**'s
      every sixth stab is Wrath of Amascut, after which what it hit takes 25% more for 6 seconds;
      **of the sun** heals you on each kill, hits a quarter harder below a quarter of your
      hitpoints, and every fortieth stab is Tumeken's Light, healing you to full; and **of
      amascut** has all of it but Tumeken's Light. You carry one keris at most.
      The Godsword knight's **Godsword** is a bare blade until level 2, when you pick its hilt
      (Armadyl, Bandos, Saradomin or Zamorak) and with it its special attack. **Dragon claws**
      slash four times at once, the last the hardest, and the **Ivandis flail** hits vampyres and
      the undead 50% harder.
    - **Ranged**: the **Twisted bow** shoots the toughest enemy in range, and hits harder the
      more hitpoints it has (up to +150% on bosses). The **Bow of Faerdhinen** fires crystal
      arrows that fly on through what they hit. The **Toxic blowpipe** fires darts that leave
      venom in what they hit, hurting it every second; it goes for enemies without venom first
      (flying past the ones with it), so the venom spreads through a crowd. Each weapon level
      upgrades its darts: bronze, steel, mithril, rune, then dragon. From the new maps:
      the **Dragon hunter crossbow**, dragon bolts that hit bosses and elites again, and the
      **Eclipse atlatl**, darts that fly through enemies and set them burning. The **Armadyl
      crossbow**'s bolts upgrade from bronze to steel, mithril, runite, then dragon. They go
      through what they hit and one in five is enchanted (a ruby takes a share of what's left of
      its hitpoints, a diamond pierces more, a dragonstone burns), and the **Crystal bow**'s
      arrows split into three where they first hit.
    - **Magic**: **Tumeken's shadow** fires splashing shadow blasts and triples your damage
      bonus for them. The **Harmonised nightmare staff** casts Fire Surge faster than anything
      else. The **Sanguinesti staff** casts blood blasts, and one hit in six heals you for half
      its damage, up to 2 hitpoints. From the new maps: the **Kodai wand**, Water Surge at two
      enemies at once, and the **Blue moon spear**, ice that crashes down and freezes. The
      **Crystal staff** grows from basic to attuned (level 4) and perfected (level 7), and
      **Toktz-mej-tal** pulses obsidian heat around you that leaves the ground burning. The
      **Volatile Nightmare staff** casts Immolate at the toughest enemy in range, slow and heavier
      the bigger the target. The **Nightmare staff**, Phosani's own, has her grasping claws come
      up out of the ground under enemies around you every few seconds, hands and all, with bosses
      and elites grabbed twice more.
- **Unlocks**: from the Desert on, every map holds new things to find. Beating it, killing one of
  its mid-run bosses, or finding something in a raid's chest, brings a weapon, a passive or a piece
  of gear into the game for every run after (a toast and the results screen say so). Until then it's
  never offered or dropped. Each map's card shows what it holds, faded until it's earned, and says
  on hover what to do for it. Everything from Lumbridge, the Barrows and the Wilderness is there
  from the start.

- **Fun maps**: the Grand Exchange, East Varrock, Falador, Last Man Standing, Castle Wars and Pest
  Control are open from the start and always endless. They have no final boss: a new round starts
  every fifteen minutes, tougher like an endless run's, and bosses come at random from each map's
  pool (the first at four minutes, then every three to five). Everyone there is another player,
  drawn in real gear (a few outfits per kind), and they say things over their heads; rangers and
  mages shoot, weakly. In Falador everyone plays Durial321, who answers back. Castle Wars has both
  teams, who fight each other too, and a Flag carrier who runs from you; Pest Control is the Void
  Knight's island and its pests, and its bosses are the four portals, which open together behind
  shields that drop one by one.
- **Boss fights**: a map that's one fight (Phosani's Nightmare, Justiciar Zachariah, Beneath Cursed
  Sands, Awakened Vardorvis), on the map screen's Bosses tab, open once the map before it has been beaten. Its boss rises as the run begins, there are
  no picks and no items (its adventurer's kit is the whole run), and killing it wins the run as any
  final boss does: it counts as a win on the map, with its quest and its hard mode. It's never
  endless.
- **Hard mode**: the switch beside Endless, open once you've beaten the map (from the start on fun
  maps). Enemies have more hitpoints and hit harder, more of them come at once, every elite is two,
  and bosses enrage at 65% of their hitpoints and attack 15% faster. How much tougher depends on the map: the early ones gain the most, as a player who's
  bought everything walks through them, and the late ones the least. Experience from bones is 10%
  lower, as the bigger crowd drops more, and coins are worth 25% more. It can be endless too, with
  records of its own. A win counts as a win on the map, and turns its tick gold.
- **Late endless caskets**: past fifteen minutes in any endless run, fun maps included, a casket that
  would drop only has a 25% chance to, and only 25 can lie on the ground at once. A round's master
  casket skips the chance but not the limit. Past the limit you get "You have a funny feeling there
  would've been a chest".
- **Auto alch** (off by default, in Settings and the pause menu) high alchs every casket as it's
  opened, gear and rare finds included, with a line saying what it was and no casket screen. It's
  the same as clicking High alch: alching a piece of a set you've started stops that set too.
- **Auto equip** (off by default, in Settings and the pause menu) equips casket gear into empty
  slots, or replaces gear only if no stat bonus decreases and at least one increases, including
  Armoury and endless upgrades. Equal gear, trade-offs and utility items still ask. Replacing a
  worn set piece always asks, even before its set bonus is active or when upgrading within the
  same set. Replaced gear is high alched as usual, with a line saying what was equipped and alched.
  Auto alch takes priority if both settings are on.
- **Elites** (golden, bigger, about one a minute) drop **reward caskets**, and any enemy can
  drop one now and then. They come in clue scroll tiers (easy, medium, hard, elite, and master
  from bosses) and get better as the run goes on; a loot beam marks them, and an arrow at the
  edge of the screen points to ones out of view. Walk over one to open it: it rattles, bursts
  open and gives some coins and one item, which you equip, keep, use or high alch for coins.
- **Power-ups** drop now and then from anything that isn't a boss: the longer it's been since the
  last one, the likelier a kill drops one, so they turn up every couple of minutes early on and
  late. A cyan beam marks them; walk over one to use it there and then, before it blinks out
  after 30 seconds. A RuneLite-style circle over each one drains from green to orange to red as
  that time runs out (the Power-up timers setting, on by default, in Settings and the pause
  menu). Once a Magnet pulls one towards you it can't run out, and its circle goes. Their damage
  keeps up with the enemies' hitpoints, and the same one never drops twice in a row. The shop's
  Runecraft upgrade makes them drop 15% more often for each rank, up to three.
    - **Dwarf multicannon**: sets up by you and shoots the nearest enemies for 20 seconds.
    - **Book of the Dead**: raises a Greater Ghost, Skeleton or Zombie thrall to fight beside you
      for 30 seconds. The ghost and skeleton shoot from your side; the zombie goes after things.
    - **Bot nuke**: everything on the map that isn't a boss dies (bosses lose a tenth of their
      hitpoints) and leaves its bones and drops behind.
    - **Magnet**: every drop on the ground flies to you, caskets and power-ups included.
    - **Giant stopwatch**: everything but bosses stands still for 8 seconds, even what turns up
      meanwhile.
    - **Sled**: you ride at 2.2 times your speed for 8 seconds and nothing can hurt you, poison and
      bosses included. It shoves enemies aside without hurting them, your weapons keep firing, and
      it frees you if you're frozen. Effect duration doesn't stretch it.
    - **Giant rune essence**: a moment after you pick it up, a huge rock lands on the thickest
      crowd within 12 tiles, marked in cyan. It hits everything within 5 tiles and throws
      survivors back, and bosses lose 8% of their hitpoints. It can't hurt you.
    - **Evil Bob**: a giant Evil Bob charges through the thickest crowd three times, over walls and
      all. Everything in his way is hit once a pass and thrown aside.
    - **Fairy ring**: a ring opens at your feet for 12 seconds. Anything that isn't a boss and comes
      within 2.5 tiles is warped far away, behind you if you're on the move. It stays where it
      opened, and shots still come through.
    - **Drill Demon**: "Run laps, maggots!" For 9 seconds everything within 12 tiles runs round you
      in a big circle, faster than usual, and can't attack, though shots already on their way still
      land. Bosses, what can't move and flag carriers carry on.
- **Trick or treat**: on Hallowed Night, enemies drop a pumpkin every so often, with an orange beam.
  Walk over it: it's a treat (a slice of pie that heals, coins, or 15 seconds of a sugar rush) or a
  trick (four ghosts, or a curse that slows you for 6 seconds). Now and then there's a halloween
  mask or a Scythe inside, for the Collection log. Those do nothing in a run.
- **Pets**: a pet found as a drop (the Little Nightmare from The Nightmare, Lil' Zik from
  Verzik with its looks from expert clears, and the Dark squirrel from Justiciar Zachariah) can be
  taken along, picked under the adventurers. It
  follows just behind you, keeping clear of your thralls, on every map. Most are only for looks,
  and in co-op the party sees each other's. **Butch**, from Awakened Vardorvis, fights: every 6
  seconds, with an enemy within 5 tiles of you, he leaps on the nearest and hits everything round
  where he lands (a tile and a half), about what a power-up does, growing as the enemies do. Not in
  boss fights, where your kit is the whole run.
- **Random events** come every two or three minutes, the first between 1:30 and 2:30, and never the
  same one twice in a row. Most are someone appearing 7 to 11 tiles away, with an orange beam, a
  minimap dot and an arrow if they're off screen. They wait 30 seconds, blinking for the last 5, and
  walking up to them sets off what they came for.
    - **Mysterious Old Man** (the likeliest): sells three power-ups, with the run paused while you
      choose. Prices go up by a tenth of the base price for each minute of the run, and with the
      map's coins. Buying one doesn't delay the next drop. In co-op, now and then one of the three
      is an extra revive instead.
    - **Quiz Master**: three items that go together and one that doesn't, with the run paused. The
      odd one out wins a random power-up.
    - **Genie**: three quarters of a level's experience.
    - **Sandwich lady**: heals 40% of your hitpoints. Ignore her and she throws a baguette as she
      leaves, for 15% of your hitpoints, which can't kill you.
    - **Rick Turpentine**: drops a casket.
    - **Strange plant**: its fruit gives 30% more movement speed for 20 seconds and cures poison, with
      poison unable to touch you until it wears off.
    - **Highwayman**: from 4:00, and not while a boss is out. A boss rather than a visitor, who
      comes for you, and each Pickpocket steals a tenth of your coins. Kill him to get them back,
      along with a hard casket.
- **Maps**: every run is on one map, and beating a map (killing its final boss) opens the next.
  Enemies have more hitpoints and hit harder on each one. Pick where to go under the
  adventurers. Enemies only turn up where you could stand and walk to, never behind a wall or in

      streaks, and hit no wider than they're drawn, and her barrage flies at under twice your
      pace with a step's worth of time between shots, so it can be stepped across. And the last
      blast doesn't end it: she stands twelve
      seconds more, untouchable, with her sleepwalkers walking in, her claws on your tile and the
      lattice doubled under them, and falls when it's up. Everyone plays the **Inquisitor** there,
      made for the fight and played nowhere else; beating her brings the Nightmare staff.
    - **Justiciar Zachariah**: the Justiciar of the Owl, whose court is his weapon. Only
      Saradomin Strike hurts him, and only from close by: out of its reach the boss bar says so,
      and a faint ring shows how far it reaches. Three phases on his hitpoints, no shield and no
      totems: at 74% and 39% he kneels a moment, untouchable, and rises into the next, and the
      court wakes a little more. His own: a **Lunge**, a strip two wide and seven long the way he
      faced, which carries him down it (step off it; behind and beside him are safe), twice in a
      row from the second phase; his **Shock wave**, a blue wave along
      the ground at where you stood, which drags whoever it reaches to his feet, binds them and
      takes three slashes (sidestep it); and **Ice barrage**, a three-by-three square on your tile
      that freezes you (move). The court's: the **crossbowmen** draw and fire bolts straight down
      their rows, two lanes of three rows at a time in turn, which the pillars stop; from the
      second phase **lightning** strikes every cell of one colour of the floor, then the other
      (the cells about to be struck glow), a **knight** throws its spinning sword at where you
      stood, across the court and back the same way, and the **bridge's fire** cycles row by row,
      all out together for a beat each time round. In the verdict he leaps to the court's middle
      for **the Owl's gaze**: everything in his sight takes over half its hitpoints; the court
      brightens as he winds up, and the pillars' shadows, darkened on the floor, are safe. Then he combines: the ice and then a
      lunge at where you're stuck, lightning round you with a sword through it, both knights at
      once. Nothing in his court heals, but the Saradominist heals a little over the fight. His
      hits are small and come quickly; the gaze takes about half your hitpoints.
      **Fighting back**: a lunge or a shock wave of his that misses leaves his guard down for a
      two seconds (he glows gold, his sword clashes, and he takes double damage); his court's bolts
      hurt him too, and a lunge into a pillar stuns him, not again for 8 s after (the stars round
      his head) for longer. **Smite**, the fight's own button (Space, a controller's A, or the
      round button on a touch screen), calls a heavy blow down on him from right beside him (a
      little further while his guard's down), every four seconds, and it's doubled while his
      guard's down; a press a moment early is held till it can go off. His lightning hurts only within the
      swirl on each cell, and never him, and the knights' swords fly right across the court. A boss fight has no
      items, so no item bar, and its results have Retry.
    - **Awakened Vardorvis**: the man of the Stranglewood, awakened, quick and close. Three
      phases on his hitpoints, each a bar of its own (kneeling a moment at 70% and 35%). His
      **Strike** is a wedge in front of him; his **Dart**, a strip two wide and eight long that
      carries him down it and roots the ground he crosses, with a ring of **bleeding vines**
      bursting round where you stood; his **Swinging axes** come in from two of the square's
      corners at where you stood, across and back. From the second phase his **Strangle**, a
      tendril along the ground that drags whoever it catches to his feet and holds them for two
      blows, and **the roots rise**: the roots round you erupt. At 35% his head tears free and
      shoots from the clearing's edge (it can be cut down), and he darts twice in a row. A dart or a
      strangle that misses leaves his guard down for two seconds (double damage); a dart into one
      of his pillars sticks him there, stunned (not again for 8 s). The **roots** close in from the
      square's edge from the start, quicker over three minutes, and from 0:40 a **blood captor**
      comes up at their front every 30 seconds (two at most) and heals him while it stands.
      **Chop**, the fight's button (Space, a controller's A, or the round button on a touch screen),
      brings the sword down overhead: a heavy blow on him from within 3 tiles (4 with his guard
      down, and doubled), the same on a captor or his head in reach, and it clears the roots
      within 3 tiles of you whether he's there or not, every four seconds.
    - **The Theatre of Blood**: the **Maiden of Sugadinti**, the **Pestilent Bloat** (who only
      really takes damage while he's down), **Nylocas Vasilias**, **Sotetseg** and his maze,
      **Xarpus** and his acid, and **Verzik Vitur** in her three forms, each a bullet hell in its
      own room.
- **Gear** goes in the OSRS equipment slots (head, cape, neck, body, shield, legs, hands, feet,
  ring) and shows on your character: about ten pieces a slot, from Rune and Mystic to the
  Barrows brothers' armour, Armadyl, Ancestral, the Infernal cape, Pegasian boots and an Avernic
  defender, and a Ring of recoil that hurts whatever hits you. One piece per slot; a new one
  replaces (and alchs) the old.
- **Gear sets**: the Void knight's equipment (3 of its 4 pieces), Inquisitor's, Crystal armour,
  Torva and Virtus (2 of their 3 each) give a bonus when enough pieces are worn, and once you
  wear one piece, caskets can bring the set's missing ones: 15% of the time, if the piece is no
  more than one rarity above what the casket rolled. Alch or reroll a piece of a set you've started
  and caskets stop bringing that set for the rest of the run.
- **Utility** items go in a 4 slot inventory and are used with **1-4** (or a click): sharks and
  anglerfish, potions (Super attack, strength and defence, Stamina, Magic, Ranging, Super
  combat, Bastion, Battlemage, Overload, Divine super combat) with a timer, brews (a Saradomin
  brew heals a lot and toughens you but weakens your hits; a Zamorak brew is the other way
  round), antidotes (Antidote++ and Sanfew serum cure poison and boss drains and curses, and
  keep them off for a while), a Bonecrusher that pulls in every bone on the ground, chinchompas that blow up
  everything around you, an Antique lamp worth a level. Copies stack. In co-op a rare casket can
  hold a Prayer cape, an extra revive that's yours the moment you take it.
- Coins and the odd cooked chicken drop too.
- Enemies get tougher as the run goes on, gently at first and faster after the first few
  minutes.
- At 15:00 the map's final boss comes. Kill it to win, even if you fall in the same instant;
  once it's won, nothing more can hurt you.
- **Minimap**: the client's, north up, drawn from the map's own minimap images, with the
  client's yellow dots for enemies (orange for elites), a red one for bosses, and caskets and
  power-ups in their colours. Bosses, caskets and power-ups out of range sit on the rim.
- **Hitsplats** are the client's own sprites: red for damage (on you too), green for poison.
- **Coins** are banked when a run ends. Spend them on **Skills** (permanent bonuses, refundable).
  Melee, Ranged and Magic each have their own skill preset, ranks and prices. The Skills tabs are
  labelled **Melee preset**, **Ranged preset** and **Magic preset**. Each can allocate the
  full gold budget: 10,000 coins can fund 10,000 in skills on every class, not 10,000 split between them.
  Only the largest page's investment is reserved from the coin bank; the Skills balance shows what
  the selected class can still spend. Refunding one page never creates extra gold or changes the others.
  A run automatically uses its adventurer's combat-style preset, including in co-op. Existing shared
  skills move to Melee; the other pages start empty. All three pages are kept when exporting and
  importing a save.
  The same skills cost the same whatever order they're bought in, but each rank on a page adds a
  little to the price of everything on it, so a cheap skill can cost several times its own price
  once dear ones are bought.
  Click or tap a bought skill's arrow button to refund one rank, even when maxed. It gives back what
  that rank adds to the page, which is its own price and what it makes the others cost. Skills
  bought at older prices give back their share of what was paid.
  On desktop, right-clicking its card does the same; touch long presses do not refund.
  **Refund all** returns the selected page's remaining investment and resets only that class's skills.
  **Woodcutting** adds melee knockback, and every other level of a melee weapon raises it too.
  **Firemaking** gives up to 3 banishes a run. Banish a new weapon or passive on a level up and
  its card greys out (pick another; the last one left can't be banished), and it's never offered
  again that run. Banish a casket's item and it's alched, and never comes again (a set's piece
  banishes the whole set). Nothing you have can be banished: not what you're levelling, carrying
  or wearing, nor a piece of a set you're wearing.
  **Smithing** and **Crafting** are the dear ones: up to 2 more weapon slots and 2 more passive
  slots.
  (The Dwarf engineer is out of the game for now; its cannon is a power-up. Saves keep it
  unlocked.)
- The **Armoury** (on the title screen) upgrades gear for good, with banked coins. Every piece
  that's come out of a casket is there; each upgrade takes it a step along its line, and from
  then on it comes out of every casket as what it's become. Most lines go up through better
  pieces, the real OSRS upgrades where there are any: an Amulet of strength becomes an Amulet of
  power, then of torture; a Fire cape an Infernal cape; Recipe for Disaster's gloves go mithril
  to barrows; Ranger boots become Pegasian boots. They end in a variant of the best piece with a
  new look and a quarter more of its stats: an ornament kit (Amulet of torture (or), Bandos
  chestplate (or)), an imbue (Berserker ring (i)), Twisted ancestral, Masori (f), the Avernic
  treads, the next tier of a diary cloak or ring. An upgrade costs 250 to 3,500 coins by the
  rarity of what it becomes, and an upgrade into a piece still locked behind a map task waits
  for it. Each piece has its own upgrades: upgrading the Amulet of strength leaves the Amulet of
  power as it was.
- **Co-op** (Multiplayer, on the title screen) is a run with up to three friends. Make a room, invite only
  (friends join with its eight-letter code or the invite link) or open to anyone browsing the
  rooms list, or join one. Everyone picks their own adventurer and brings their own skills,
  Armoury and unlocked items; the host picks the map, endless and hard mode, and starts once
  everyone (the host too) is ready, which can be a party of one. The host can open or close the room, kick a
  player and disband it. A pasted invite link in the code box leaves just its code. Everyone
  gets a readable name to play under, like "Plucky Chinchompa", and rooms get one too, like "Moonlit
  Crossing". **New name** picks another, any time but during a run. Where the server knows it,
  each player has their country's flag (and each open room its host's); **Co-op flag** in Settings
  hides yours, except while you host an open room.
    - You can only join a room on a map you've unlocked yourself (and its endless or hard mode, for
      a room on them), and the host can only pick what everyone in the room has, so nobody skips ahead. A
      server can let anyone in instead (`MULTIPLAYER_ANY_MAP`); then a win opens the map it was
      on for a player who hadn't got that far (along with the next), but not the maps before it.
    - The run's **level is shared**: every level up gives each player their own three choices, and
      the run waits for everyone to pick (and for everyone's item from a casket). Coins and kills are
      shared too, and everyone banks the run's coins.
    - A **random event's** visitor turns up by one of the party, and whoever walks up first meets
      them and gets what they bring. The Mysterious Old Man's and the Quiz Master's offers are
      theirs to choose from while the run waits, paid for from the shared coins. Ignore the
      Sandwich lady and everyone still standing gets the baguette.
    - A **boss** comes as a pair, or on hard mode one for each player, each turning up by a
      different player. Between them the copies have one boss's hitpoints and experience, and its
      loot drops with the last to fall, so a party fights more bosses rather than bigger ones.
      Every copy of a final boss must fall to win, or to move an endless run on. Slash Bash and
      The Mimic's caskets come this way too; Hallowed Night's final fight and Pest Control's
      portals stay as they are.
    - A **boss's attacks** land on whoever they reach: The Nightmare's bullets, marks, quarters and
      charge, Slash Bash's pools and The Mimic's stomp hurt each player caught in them, and the blast
      once her Sleepwalkers reach her hits everyone. A curse,
      her parasite and being held are on the player they hit. Her fight starts with the whole
      party in the middle of her square, and each player rolls for their own Collection log drop.
    - Anyone who keeps the run waiting for 20 seconds (a level up, a casket, a random event's
      offer) has it decided for them: the first reward, taking the casket, or walking away. The
      host can kick a player from the menu mid-run, and everyone has to be on the same version of
      the game.
    - A casket gives every player who's up an item of their own (rolled on their own luck, gear
      and banishes), whoever picks it up, and the run waits for each of them. Its coins go to the
      shared pot once. Ordinary kills drop fewer caskets the bigger the party (x0.69 for two,
      x0.43 for four), as a party kills more, so each player gets about as many items as on their
      own, and the party about as many casket coins. Power-ups hit as hard against the tougher
      crowd. The Highwayman takes his share of the shared coins once, however many of you he hits.
    - The crowd is **bigger and tougher** for each player past the first: 35% more of them about
      at once, 50% more hitpoints, bosses 110% more (keeping up with the party's damage), and
      10% harder hits. Bones are worth 12% less for each, so with the bigger crowd the party
      levels about as fast as a player on their own (worth more when the crowd is at its cap and
      can grow no bigger). It eases off as players go down or leave, each still counting as half
      a player, so the last one of four standing faces a crowd made for two and a half.
    - Enemies go for whoever's **nearest**, and pickups fly to whoever's nearest.
    - A player who falls with no ring of life left (it saves you as it does on your own) is
      **down**, lying where they fell and watching a teammate who's still up. The run goes
      on while anyone's standing, without waiting on the downed for level ups, and ends when
      everyone's down.
    - A teammate can **revive** a downed player by standing over them for 5 seconds (the Prayer
      cape's emote, with a bar filling up over the fallen; stepping away starts over). Each
      player can be revived once a run, plus once more for each **Prayer cape** they find: a
      rare casket item, or now and then for sale from the Mysterious Old Man, both only in
      co-op. They come back with 75% of their hitpoints, then 50%, then 25% for every revive
      after, and with none left they're down for good. For 5 seconds a revived player can't
      be hurt, and enemies leave them alone for a teammate instead (unless nobody else is
      up); food, coins and caskets are theirs to pick up all the while. Level ups the party had
      while they were down are theirs to choose once they're back, but a casket picked up
      meanwhile has no item for them.
    - The pause menu doesn't stop a co-op run. Leaving it takes you out of the party; the host
      leaving ends it for everyone.
    - Teammates show with their names in green, an arrow at the edge of the screen when they're out
      of view, green dots on the minimap and their hitpoints down the left of the screen.
    - **Movement prediction** (on by default, in Settings and the pause menu): a player who
      joined a game sees their own character move as soon as they press, then correct to the host.
      It only changes what they see: hits and pickups still follow the host.
- **Quests** (on the title screen) pay banked coins: each is named for an OSRS quest and asks
  for kills (Goblin Diplomacy, 300 goblins), a boss (Vampyre Slayer, Count Draynor; Desert
  Treasure I, Fareed, Dessous and Damis) or caskets opened (Pirate's Treasure). They count over
  as many runs as they take, on any map but the fun ones, and each pays its coins once (100 to 2,500), when the
  run that finishes it ends; one finished mid-run rings the bell. The list is coloured like the
  client's quest tab: red until started, yellow while under way, green once done. In a run, a
  tracker under the minimap (and the pause menu) shows how far the map's quests have got, and the
  ones done all over if the map has what they're after (no Ratcatchers in the Inferno).
- **Damage meter**: under the quests, DPS is the damage you've dealt from every source over the last
  8 seconds, counted the way the results screen counts it (the whole of a killing hit, and nothing
  for a blocked one).
- The **UI** grows with the window: it's laid out for one about 720 pixels tall, and drawn up
  to twice the size on bigger ones (about 1.35x in a 1080p browser window).
- **Sound**: every enemy, spell and pickup plays its OSRS sound effect, synthesized from the
  cache the way the client does it. **M** mutes; the volume is in the title screen's Settings and
  on the pause screen.
- **Camera spin**: the camera circles your character on the title screen and the menus; the
  title screen's Settings stop it (it starts off stopped if your system asks for reduced motion).
- **Power-up timers**: the circles over power-ups on the ground; Settings and the pause screen
  turn them off.
- **Puzzle helper**: in the Tombs of Amascut's Scabaras room, marks what to step on, as the ToA
  plugin does in OSRS: on at Entry, and at any raid level once you've beaten the room. Settings and
  the pause screen turn it off.
